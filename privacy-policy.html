Nept Track Privacy Policy

Effective 14 November 2026

Your data stays on your phone

Tasks, habits, alarms, money records, notes and inbox captures are saved in a database on your device. They are not uploaded to any server, and nobody else can read them. The one exception is a cloud backup that you start yourself after signing in, described under "An account is optional" below.

Uninstalling the app deletes that database. Export a backup from Settings first if you want to keep it.

If Android's own backup is switched on for your Google account, Android may include the app's data in that backup, so that it comes back on a new phone. That copy is made and kept by Google under your phone's settings (Settings › Google › Backup); we never receive it.

An account is optional

You can use every part of Nept Track without an account. The app never asks for your phone number, and no part of it, including the third-party services named below, reads or uploads your phone number.

If you choose to sign in (Settings → Premium → Sign in), you can use Google, an email address with a password, or a one-time code that we email you. With Google sign-in, Google tells us your email address, name and profile photo; we never see your Google password. A password you create is stored only as a one-way hash that we cannot read. We then store, on our server:

  • Your email address, to sign you in and to find your account again on a new phone.
  • Profile details you choose to add: a name, a profile photo, your birthday, your country, an address (a city or area is enough), a hobby and what you do (for example student or job). All of them are optional and can be changed or removed at any time. We use them to show your profile and to understand who uses the app. We do not ask for weight, height or other health details.
  • Your Nept ID (the random app ID described under Premium), so that a subscription bought on this phone follows your account, and so that one phone cannot claim an invite twice.
  • Account activity: when you last opened the app while signed in, how many times, and the app version. We use this to understand how many signed-in people use the app and to give support.
  • Cloud backups (Premium only, and only when you tap Back up now): a copy of your tasks and habits, money records, notes, captures, alarms, sketches and special dates, kept so you can restore them. Period tracker data is never included. We keep your five most recent backups.
  • Invites (only if you use them): your invite code, which account invited which, your points, and on how many days an invited person used the app. The person who invited you sees your profile name, or part of your email address, in their list of invited friends.

Without an account, the app still asks our server, each time it opens, whether this install has Premium and whether it has been blocked for abuse. That request carries the Nept ID and the app version. We do not store it.

Accounts are kept with Supabase (Supabase, Inc.; database and sign-in) and photos and backups with Cloudflare R2 (Cloudflare, Inc.; file storage). They process this data for us and do not use it for their own purposes. We do not sell it or use it for advertising.

Deleting your account. Steps are also on the Delete your account page. In the app, open Settings → Premium → Delete account. This removes your account, profile, photo and cloud backups from our server straight away and cannot be undone. The data on your phone is not touched. If you cannot open the app, email us from the address of the account and we will delete it within 30 days. A paid subscription is separate and must be cancelled in Google Play.

Anonymous usage analytics

Nept Track uses two analytics services to learn which screens people use, what confuses them and where the app breaks: Microsoft Clarity and Google Firebase Analytics. Neither one is given your name, email address, phone number, contacts or precise location.

Microsoft Clarity

  • Session recordings and heatmaps. Clarity records how you move through the app: which screens you open, where you tap, scroll and swipe, and how long you stay. These recordings are used to find confusing screens and broken buttons.
  • Masking. Recordings are set to mask the text and images on screen, so the words you write (notes, task titles, captures) and your amounts appear as blocks, not as readable content.
  • Named events. The app sends short event names such as “Tasks › tap: Add task” or “Money › sheet: New entry”. These use only the app’s own button and screen names. When a button would name something you wrote, such as a task or a note, the event says “item” instead.
  • Ad events. Which ad placement was shown, closed, rewarded or failed, so ads can be kept out of the way.
  • Crash reports. When a screen fails, the error’s name and message and the screen it happened on.
  • Device details. Phone model, Android version, screen size, app version, language, and your country or region derived from your IP address. Clarity assigns a random id to your install so sessions from the same phone can be grouped; it is not linked to who you are.

Clarity is run by Microsoft and processes this data under the Microsoft Privacy Statement.

Google Firebase Analytics

  • Counts app opens, screen views and the same named events as above (with the same “item” rule), plus a few actions such as creating a task, converting a capture or sharing a report. These carry only fixed labels like “habit” or “expense”, never your text, amounts or category names.
  • Uses an app-instance id, phone model, Android version, language and approximate country. Google processes this under Firebase’s privacy terms.
  • Premium events. If you subscribe to Premium, RevenueCat tells Firebase Analytics that a purchase, free trial, renewal, cancellation, expiry or billing problem happened, with the plan and its price. To file this under the same anonymous install, the app gives RevenueCat the Firebase app-instance id. No card or bank details are involved, and nothing you write is included.
  • Measuring our own ads. Our Firebase Analytics is linked to our Google Ads account. Google Ads receives anonymous counts of a few actions, such as a first task being created or a Premium purchase, so we can see whether our ads bring people who use the app. Using this data to personalise ads is switched off.

Turning it off

Settings › Your data › Share anonymous usage data switches off both Clarity and Firebase Analytics at once. It is on when you first install the app. While it is off, nothing is recorded or sent, the app-instance id is not given to RevenueCat, and one stored earlier is removed.

AI assistant (optional)

Ask Nept answers questions about your own habits and spending using Google Gemini. It is off until you read what it shares and turn it on. You do not need an account to use it.

When you ask a question, the app sends our server a summary it computes on your phone: habit names and how often you did them, the titles of open to-dos, spending totals per category, budgets and inbox counts, together with your question. Our server passes this to Google Gemini, which writes the answer, and sends the answer back. Your notes, your captures, transaction notes and individual transactions are never sent.

Our server does not keep your questions, the summary or the answers. To hold a daily limit on questions, it keeps only a count per day against your account or Nept ID, and, when you are not signed in, a scrambled form of your network address that cannot be turned back into the address.

Conversations are not saved by the app. Google processes requests under its own terms (Gemini API terms). Turn the assistant off at any time in Settings › Preferences › Ask Nept.

Advertising

Nept Track shows ads from Google AdMob: banners and ads inside longer lists, a full-screen ad now and then at a natural break such as leaving an insights screen, and sometimes one when you come back to the app. Screens with little of your own content show no ads, an ad never appears when you leave the app, and a full-screen ad is never shown while an alarm is ringing. Ads never appear inside the task, alarm, money or note editors.

Google may use your device's advertising ID and approximate location, derived from your IP address, to show ads that are more relevant to you. Where the law requires it, you are asked for consent first and can change your choice in Settings. You can reset or delete the advertising ID in your phone's settings.

Nothing you write, and none of your amounts, tasks or notes, is used to choose ads. See how Google uses data in ads.

Push messages

The app registers with Google Firebase Cloud Messaging so it can receive occasional announcements, such as a new feature. This gives your phone an anonymous device token; no message content you write is sent. You can block these in your phone's notification settings.

Camera and microphone

Camera. The app opens the camera only when you choose to take a photo for a note. The photo is saved on your phone with that note. It is not uploaded, and it is not part of a cloud backup.

Microphone. The app listens only while you use voice input, in search or in Ask Nept, and shows when it is listening. Your speech is turned into text by your phone's speech service (on Android, Google's), which may process the audio on Google's servers under Google's privacy policy. The app receives only the text and never records, keeps or sends audio itself.

Android asks for each permission the first time you use the feature. Both are optional: the rest of the app works without them, and you can take them back in your phone's settings.

Reminders

Reminders are scheduled by your phone and stay on your phone. Turning notifications off in Settings stops them immediately.

If you pick one of your phone's own tones for a reminder or an alarm, the app stores only a link to that tone. It does not read, copy or upload your sound files.

Alarms

Alarms you set in Nept Track are stored and scheduled on your phone. To ring on time, even with the app closed or the phone locked, the app uses Android's alarm permissions: exact alarms, full-screen alerts over the lock screen, and a short-lived foreground service that plays the tone and vibrates until you snooze or dismiss it. None of this sends anything off your phone.

Focus time and widgets

If you give the app Do Not Disturb access, it switches your phone to Do Not Disturb during the focus blocks you set, and switches it back afterwards. It does not read your notifications or calls.

Home-screen widgets show a summary of your tasks, money and notes. That summary is kept on your phone for the widget to draw.

App lock

If you turn on the app lock, your phone checks your fingerprint, face or screen lock itself. Nept Track never sees or stores your biometric data.

Your choices

Export your data as a file at any time from Settings, and import it again later.

Erase everything from Settings. That is immediate and cannot be undone.

Children

Nept Track is not directed at children under 13 and does not knowingly collect data from them.

Premium subscription (optional)

Nept Track Premium is a paid subscription sold through Google Play. Google Play handles the payment; we never see your card or bank details. To check whether your subscription is active, the app uses RevenueCat (RevenueCat, Inc., USA), which receives a random app ID created on your phone (shown in Settings as your Nept ID) and the Google Play purchase record. It does not receive your name, email, phone number or anything you write in the app. While anonymous usage data is on, RevenueCat also receives the Firebase app-instance id, as described under Google Firebase Analytics above. See the RevenueCat privacy policy. You can cancel any time in Google Play.

Period tracker (optional)

If you choose "Woman" in the app, it can track your period. The dates, symptoms, mood and notes you log stay only on your phone, and in a backup file only if you export one yourself. They are left out of cloud backups. They are never sent to us, to analytics, to advertisers or to the AI assistant. Predictions are estimates for planning, not medical advice or birth control.

Support

Support is by email, or by live chat. The chat is a web page run by Crisp (Crisp IM SAS, France), shown inside the app when you tap Live chat; the app contains no chat software (SDK), cannot read your phone number or contacts for the chat, and sends Crisp nothing on its own. Before it opens the first time, the app tells you this and asks you to continue. So that your conversation is still there next time, the app creates a random chat ID when you first open the chat, keeps it on your phone, and gives it to Crisp with the chat page; the chat page may also keep its own data in the app's web storage on your phone. The chat ID says nothing about who you are, and erasing the app's data removes it. Messages and any contact details you choose to type in the chat are handled by Crisp under the Crisp privacy policy. Nothing is sent until you open the chat.
